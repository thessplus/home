title: The One With the Wrong Promotion
date: 2026-11-20

description: A great employee doesn't automatically make a great manager. Here's
  why thoughtful promotions matter, especially for small businesses.
---
What if promoting your best employee turns out to be one of your worst business decisions? The **Peter Principle**, introduced by Laurence J. Peter, describes how employees can be promoted based on past success until they reach roles they are no longer equipped to perform. For small and medium-sized businesses, promoting someone who knows the job, understands the business, and has been around for years may seem like the most practical choice, especially when resources are limited. But without the right preparation, businesses risk losing a great performer while creating problems in team productivity, morale, and overall performance.

**Promotion and reward are two different things**, and knowing the business does not automatically mean someone is ready or willing to manage people. Before promoting, businesses should consider the role's requirements, the employee's competencies and career interests, and whether training and support can bridge any gaps. When promotion is necessary, clear expectations, proper preparation, and ongoing guidance can help employees succeed, while better compensation and recognition can reward those who prefer to remain specialists. ***What's the Plus side? Understanding the Peter Principle helps us set employees up for success, not failure.***
